# Max Questions For A Demo Call

Use this when the prospect is ready to see Cranston, has a test account, or is evaluating the product with a specific workflow in mind. Each question is broken into three lines: what to ask, what to listen for, and what the answer should help you decide.

Transcript-backed example: see `peter-paula-burgos-drake-review-intake-exact-quote-bank.md` in this folder for a Drake demo that moved into a 20-return pilot and software install next step.

## Set The Frame

Question: "Can you see my screen?"
Listen for: whether everyone can follow the walkthrough.
Decide: whether to start the demo or pause for access issues.

Question: "Before I show this, remind me what workflow you're trying to improve."
Listen for: tax prep, review cleanup, document issues, bookkeeping classification, matching, or reconciliation.
Decide: which demo path to emphasize.

Question: "Do you want to keep your current portal and practice management system?"
Listen for: love for TaxDome or other portals versus desire to replace them.
Decide: whether to frame Cranston as a prep layer or broader platform.

Question: "What would you want your reviewer to see before trusting the output?"
Listen for: source links, workpapers, year-over-year comparisons, diagnostics, or field-level confidence.
Decide: which controls to show first.

## Show Documents And Intake

Question: "Does this document upload flow match how your team receives client files?"
Listen for: PDFs, scans, spreadsheets, organizer exports, prior returns, client portal downloads, or messy packages.
Decide: whether the package story feels realistic.

Question: "Would an admin be the person uploading documents, or would a preparer own this step?"
Listen for: handoff model and skill level of the first user.
Decide: where Cranston should enter their workflow.

Question: "If a client uploads the wrong year or misses a form, how do you catch that today?"
Listen for: manual review, preparer intuition, prior-year comparison, or reviewer discovery.
Decide: whether to linger on findings and missing-item detection.

## Show Findings

Question: "Does that interface make sense?"
Listen for: whether they understand high, medium, low, and informational flags.
Decide: whether to keep going or explain the review model more simply.

Question: "When something is missing, would you request it from the client or push the return forward without it?"
Listen for: firm risk tolerance and reviewer workflow.
Decide: whether to show client requests, dismissing findings, or exporting with known gaps.

Question: "When something is handwritten or messy, what would you want the system to do?"
Listen for: flag for review, extract anyway, ignore, or require human judgment.
Decide: how to position uncertainty and professional control.

Question: "Are these the kinds of issues your team usually finds before review, or during review?"
Listen for: where rework currently happens.
Decide: whether Cranston's value is preparer speed or reviewer cleanup reduction.

## Show Draft And Source Evidence

Question: "Is this the kind of line-by-line view your reviewer would trust?"
Listen for: need for a workpaper summary, field list, spreadsheet view, source links, or tax form preview.
Decide: which evidence view matters most.

Question: "Do you want one page with all the numbers, or do you prefer clicking through field by field?"
Listen for: spreadsheet-oriented review, tax-form-oriented review, or exception-only review.
Decide: how to present the draft tab and source evidence.

Question: "Does seeing the exact source behind each number solve the checking problem?"
Listen for: confidence, skepticism, or requests for more audit trail.
Decide: whether source traceability is the buying hook.

Question: "Would you check all of this before export, or only the flagged items?"
Listen for: full review, sampled review, exception review, or role-based review.
Decide: how aggressive the workflow can be in a pilot.

## Show Year-Over-Year And Judgment

Question: "Where should the prior-year context come from for a new client?"
Listen for: uploaded prior return, prior workpaper, Drake, Lacerte, or no prior-year file.
Decide: whether prior-year comparison is available for the test.

Question: "When the system sees something changed year over year, who decides whether that is okay?"
Listen for: preparer, reviewer, partner, tax lead, or client follow-up.
Decide: who needs to be involved in the next review.

Question: "Do you have firm-level practices the model should follow?"
Listen for: capitalization thresholds, depreciation practices, review preferences, client-specific rules, or organizer standards.
Decide: whether to discuss instructions and onboarding.

Question: "Would you want those practices applied across all returns?"
Listen for: standardization needs and partner-level approval.
Decide: whether firm instructions are part of the pilot setup.

## Show Export And Integration

Question: "Which tax software environment would this need to land in for the test to be meaningful?"
Listen for: buyer's actual engine, hosted setup, duplicate client, test client, or web-only proof.
Decide: whether the next proof is analysis-only, controlled output, or live writeback.

Question: "Who controls installation in that environment?"
Listen for: internal IT, hosting provider, firm admin, tax lead, or external vendor.
Decide: who must be included before promising integration timing.

Question: "Would you want to edit in Cranston after export, or in the tax software?"
Listen for: reviewer habits and comfort with destination software.
Decide: how to describe post-export control.

Question: "Does Drake / Lacerte need to be open for your team to feel comfortable with this workflow?"
Listen for: concerns from Juno or computer-use tools.
Decide: whether to emphasize direct connection and parallel work.

## Demo Bookkeeping When Relevant

Question: "Do your clients use QuickBooks Online?"
Listen for: QBO coverage and whether bookkeeping is a real opportunity.
Decide: whether to show bookkeeping automation at all.

Question: "Is the issue classification, matching, reconciliation, or cleanup after third-party apps push data into QuickBooks?"
Listen for: the exact job to be automated.
Decide: whether Cranston can address it now or needs technical follow-up.

Question: "What app creates the bills, receipts, or payments?"
Listen for: Toast, CCC, Jobber, payment processors, bill pay tools, or industry-specific apps.
Decide: what Max needs to take back to the tech team.

Question: "Would you be able to show us that workflow on a follow-up call?"
Listen for: willingness to screen-share real operations.
Decide: whether to schedule a technical discovery/demo.

## Close The Demo

Question: "Does this look like something that might be a fit?"
Listen for: readiness, objections, test interest, or missing proof.
Decide: whether to move to trial, pilot, or another stakeholder call.

Question: "What would one return need to prove for your team to use this again?"
Listen for: time savings, accuracy, fewer review notes, clean export, source traceability, or reduced offshore work.
Decide: success criteria for the test.

Question: "Can we upload one or two returns together, then review the output on a separate call?"
Listen for: agreement to a real proof process.
Decide: schedule the upload call and output-review call.

Question: "Can you pick a return that is moderately complex and already completed?"
Listen for: whether the prospect has a known-answer comparison.
Decide: whether the first test will be fair and useful.

Question: "Who needs to be on the output-review call?"
Listen for: operator, reviewer, tax lead, owner, or technical person.
Decide: who must be invited.

Question: "What time works next week?"
Listen for: exact availability.
Decide: the calendar step before ending the call.
