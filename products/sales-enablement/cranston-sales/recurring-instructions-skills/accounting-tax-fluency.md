# Accounting And Tax Fluency

Use this before writing sales copy, preparing for calls, or interpreting a firm's tax workflow. The goal is not to sound like a CPA. The goal is to understand the handoffs well enough to ask grounded questions.

## Mental Model

- Start with the underlying business activity, then follow it through books, financial statements, workpapers, and the tax return.
- Treat tax preparation as controlled conversion of incomplete evidence into a filing a professional will sign.
- Remember that cash, book profit, and taxable income can differ. Do not collapse them into one idea.
- Assume "the books are not tax-ready" means the firm still has to fix, reconcile, classify, or support the accounting record before it can feed a return.

## Listen For These Workflows

- Source activity: bank activity, invoices, receipts, payroll, loans, contracts, brokerage statements, W-2s, 1099s, K-1s, and prior returns.
- Books: general ledger, trial balance, chart of accounts, reconciliations, close process, and adjusting journal entries.
- Workpapers: the bridge between source documents, books, judgments, adjustments, and tax categories.
- Tax engine: the software that calculates forms, diagnostics, states, e-file output, and return status.

## Translate Common Call Language

- "1040 with a C and E rentals" means an individual return with a sole-proprietor business and rental activities.
- "K-1s flow to E" means pass-through entity results feed the owner's individual return.
- "Roll forward SALY" means start from last year's return or workpapers and update the current year.
- "Tie it out" means prove that two records agree.
- "Clear open items" means resolve missing documents, unsupported treatments, unanswered questions, and review notes.
- "Run diagnostics" means use the tax engine's warnings and errors as checks, not as a replacement for review.

## Questions To Carry Into Calls

- What return type is the firm preparing: 1040, 1065, 1120-S, 1120, or a mix?
- What evidence feeds the return: client documents, books, trial balance, general ledger, prior return, workpapers, or K-1s?
- Where do the books come from, and are they already reconciled and tax-ready?
- What workpapers does the preparer build before review?
- Which tax software calculates the return, and where does the firm want Cranston to land output?
- What slows review: missing source documents, unreconciled books, mapping, fixed assets, diagnostics, or judgment calls?
