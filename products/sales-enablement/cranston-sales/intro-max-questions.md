# Max Questions For A First Intro Call

Use this when the prospect has not seen Cranston yet, or when you still need to understand the firm before deciding what to demo. Each question is broken into three lines: what to ask, what to listen for, and what the answer should help you decide.

Transcript-backed example: see `max-laura-call-1-exact-quote-bank.md` in this folder for the exact question sequence Max used with Laura Mcclure-fleck, plus Laura's objections, Max's responses, and ICP signals from that call.

## Open The Call

Question: "How are you?" / "Where are you calling from?"
Listen for: local context, energy level, whether they are rushed, and whether multiple stakeholders are joining.
Decide: how quickly to move from rapport into discovery.

Question: "I'm excited to learn more about you and your practice. You do tax or accounting work in [city], is that right?"
Listen for: CPA firm, tax-only shop, bookkeeping firm, wealth/tax hybrid, or full-service advisory firm.
Decide: whether to frame Cranston around tax prep, bookkeeping, capacity, or a broader workflow.

Question: "You run more of a full-service firm, is that right?"
Listen for: wealth management, estate planning, bookkeeping, tax resolution, advisory, or pure compliance.
Decide: whether the buyer cares only about prep automation or also about client context and planning opportunities.

## Map The Firm

Question: "What do you focus on primarily?"
Listen for: tax season work versus year-round services like bookkeeping, resolution, advisory, or wealth.
Decide: which workflow is the anchor for the call.

Question: "How many tax returns do you do in an average year?"
Listen for: scale, staffing pressure, and whether the firm is big enough for a meaningful pilot.
Decide: whether to discuss a small test, a 25-50 return pilot, or next-season volume.

Question: "What's the mix of business and personal?"
Listen for: 1040-heavy, entity-heavy, or mixed practice.
Decide: which return type to demo or recommend for the first test.

Question: "What kinds of business returns do you do most: 1120S, 1120, or 1065?"
Listen for: entity mix and whether the prospect is inside Cranston's current support corridor.
Decide: whether to keep the demo in supported forms or flag a boundary early.

Question: "Are the clients fairly straightforward, or do you have more complex returns or a specialty?"
Listen for: Schedule E, Schedule C, K-1s, multi-state, rentals, trading, military, or high-volume simple W-2 returns.
Decide: how complex the test return should be.

Question: "How many people are on the team?"
Listen for: preparers, reviewers, admins, partners, and operational leverage.
Decide: who should be trained and who needs to trust the output.

Question: "Do you use offshore support?"
Listen for: capacity relief, rework, quality issues, cost pressure, or desire to reduce offshore roles.
Decide: whether to frame Cranston as prep capacity, reviewer leverage, or offshore replacement.

## Understand The Current Workflow

Question: "What software do you use to file taxes?"
Listen for: Drake, Lacerte, CCH Axcess, UltraTax, or another engine.
Decide: whether integration is straightforward, environment-specific, or a potential blocker.

Question: "Do you use any practice management tools or a portal?"
Listen for: TaxDome, Canopy, email, Drive, OneDrive, or firm-specific intake.
Decide: whether to position Cranston as a prep layer or as a broader workflow replacement.

Question: "How do you collect information from clients?"
Listen for: organizers, uploaded PDFs, scans, spreadsheets, prior returns, client portals, and messy intake patterns.
Decide: whether to emphasize document classification, missing-item detection, or source traceability.

Question: "Are you hosted, or is the tax software local?"
Listen for: Verito, Ace Cloud, local desktop, remote desktop, or firm-managed IT.
Decide: who has to be involved for setup.

Question: "Are you switching systems? If so, why?"
Listen for: dissatisfaction, mergers, platform consolidation, or tax-season readiness.
Decide: whether to sell into the current workflow or the future workflow.

## Find The Pain

Question: "Tell me about your search for capacity."
Listen for: data entry, source hunting, client missing items, review notes, offshore rework, or staff constraints.
Decide: which pain to repeat back before showing product.

Question: "What are you trying to automate or streamline?"
Listen for: prep, bookkeeping, classification, matching, reconciliation, review, or client requests.
Decide: whether this is a tax call, a bookkeeping call, or a technical investigation.

Question: "Have you evaluated any other AI tools?"
Listen for: TaxGPT, Juno, GruntWorx, SurePrep, Magnetic, Instead, Botkeeper, or internal tools.
Decide: which comparison matters and what objection is likely.

Question: "What did you like or not like about those tools?"
Listen for: too many steps, unclear pricing, extra review burden, poor integration, or outsourcing disguised as automation.
Decide: what Cranston needs to prove differently.

Question: "Where does the work get stuck today?"
Listen for: intake, missing docs, prep, tax-software entry, review, bookkeeping cleanup, or handoffs.
Decide: which part of the demo should get the most time.

Question: "Who does that work today?"
Listen for: admin, junior preparer, offshore team, reviewer, partner, or owner.
Decide: who should operate Cranston in the first test.

## Qualify The Next Step

Question: "Does this look like something that might be a fit for you?"
Listen for: clear yes, curiosity, skepticism, or a request to test.
Decide: whether to propose a trial account, pilot, or further technical discovery.

Question: "Do you have extensions you're still working on?"
Listen for: active real returns that can be used before next tax season.
Decide: whether there is a near-term pilot path.

Question: "Would you be able to find a couple returns to test?"
Listen for: access to completed returns, client authorization, complexity, and reviewer familiarity.
Decide: what to ask them to bring to the next call.

Question: "Could you use one that's moderately complex and familiar to you?"
Listen for: whether they understand that the first test should be representative, not impossible.
Decide: whether the test will create a clean comparison.

Question: "Who should be on the output-review call?"
Listen for: operator, reviewer, tax lead, firm owner, or technical contact.
Decide: who must attend for the test to convert into a pilot.

Question: "What time works next week?"
Listen for: specific availability and stakeholder schedules.
Decide: the calendar step before ending the call.
