# Max Prospect Call Flow Guide

Based on Max's August 11, 2026 prospect calls with Erica Whatley, Steven Nuckols / Elizabeth at Wealth Compass, and Rachel Dykstra. This is a question-led flow, not a script. The goal is to sound like Max: curious, conversational, specific, and willing to adapt the demo to the prospect's actual workflow.

## 1. Open Warmly And Locate The Person

Goal: make the call feel human before moving into discovery. Max does not overdo rapport; he uses it to create ease, then pivots quickly into the business.

Ask:
- "How are you?"
- "Where are you calling from?"
- "I'm excited to learn more about you and your practice."
- "I'm assuming you do some sort of tax or accounting work in [city], is that right?"
- "You run more of a full-service financial firm, is that right?"

Listen for:
- Location and local context.
- Whether they are a CPA firm, tax-only shop, wealth/tax hybrid, bookkeeping firm, or broader advisory firm.
- Who is on the call and what role they play.

Move on when:
- You know what kind of firm they are.
- You know whether there are multiple stakeholders, especially tax leadership, operations, or technology.

## 2. Map The Practice

Goal: understand the size, services, and return mix before talking product.

Ask:
- "What do you focus on primarily?"
- "How many tax returns do you do in an average year?"
- "What's the mix of business and personal?"
- "What kinds of business returns do you do most: 1120S, 1120, 1065?"
- "Is the majority individual returns?"
- "Are the clients fairly straightforward, or do you have more complex returns or a specialty?"
- "How many people do you have on the team?"
- "Do you have offshore support?"
- "Is that an offshore bookkeeping or prep team?"
- "India or Philippines?"

Listen for:
- Return count and complexity.
- 1040 versus entity mix.
- Number of preparers, reviewers, admins, and offshore staff.
- Whether the pain is capacity, quality, cost, or review leverage.

Max pattern:
- He often makes a light, informed guess, then asks them to confirm. Example: "With that many returns, you must have at least six to ten people onshore, right?"
- This makes discovery feel consultative instead of like an intake form.

## 3. Identify The Current Stack And Workflow

Goal: find the operational corridor before making any product promise.

Ask:
- "What software do you use to file taxes?"
- "Do you use any practice management tools or a portal?"
- "How do you collect information from clients?"
- "Are you hosted, or is your tax software running locally?"
- "Which hosting environment are you in?"
- "Do your bookkeeping clients use QuickBooks Online?"
- "Is Drake / Lacerte / TaxDome doing enough for you today?"
- "Are you switching systems? If so, why?"

Listen for:
- Tax engine: Drake, Lacerte, CCH Axcess, UltraTax, etc.
- Hosting: local desktop, Verito, Ace Cloud, other hosted setups.
- Client portal and intake system: TaxDome, Canopy, email, Drive, etc.
- Whether Cranston should layer on top of their workflow or replace part of it.

Safe positioning:
- "If you love TaxDome and want to keep it, that's fine. If you hate it and need to move, we can talk about the broader platform."
- "Support depends on the form, software, edition, hosting, and workflow. We'll confirm that corridor before the test."

## 4. Understand Why They Are Looking Now

Goal: get the prospect to say the pain in their own words before the demo.

Ask:
- "Tell me a little bit about your search for AI tools."
- "How did you find us?"
- "What can I help you with potentially?"
- "Tell me about your search for capacity."
- "What are you trying to automate or streamline?"
- "Have you found anything that's good?"
- "Are there any features that excited you about using AI?"
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "What did you not like about that?"
- "What's the pricing or packaging like on the tools you've looked at?"

Listen for:
- Grunt work, data entry, source hunting, client document issues, offshore quality, review bottlenecks.
- Competitors already evaluated.
- Buying trigger: capacity, staff reduction, replacing Juno/GruntWorx/SurePrep, tax season urgency, bookkeeping cleanup.
- Economic pain: onshore labor doing low-value work, offshore creating rework, credit-based pricing uncertainty.

Max pattern:
- He repeats the pain back in concrete terms.
- He connects with prior practitioner experience when useful, but keeps the prospect's problem at the center.
- He asks enough before demoing so the demo feels tailored.

## 5. Drill Into The Bottleneck

Goal: define the specific workflow step where Cranston must prove value.

Ask:
- "Where is the biggest time suck: information gathering, data input, preparation, review, or cleanup?"
- "Who does that work today?"
- "What does the reviewer check first?"
- "What kind of returns create the most reviewer cleanup?"
- "Are the books clean, or is the tax team repairing the package before prep?"
- "If clients upload the wrong thing or miss something, how do you catch that today?"
- "When something is handwritten or messy, what do you want the system to do?"
- "What decisions should an admin handle versus a preparer or reviewer?"

For bookkeeping-specific pain, ask:
- "Are you saying the third-party app creates the bill in QuickBooks, then the credit card transaction needs to match against that bill?"
- "What app is generating the bills or receipts?"
- "Which apps are most painful?"
- "Is the issue really inside QuickBooks after the data has already landed there?"
- "Is matching the problem, or classification, or reconciliation?"

Listen for:
- The exact repeated task they want removed.
- Whether the work requires an API, a tax-software integration, judgment, or just automation.
- Whether Max should sell tax prep, bookkeeping automation, or a future technical investigation.

## 6. Transition To Demo With Permission

Goal: make the demo feel like the answer to their stated problem, not a generic tour.

Ask:
- "I can show you a demo if you'd like to see what our software can do."
- "Do you mind if I show you a little bit of our tax program?"
- "Does that make sense?"
- "Can you see my screen?"

Transition language:
- "Let me tell you a little bit about our product."
- "The way to think about this is: upload the full package, let the model reason over it, then review what it is about to push before it goes into the tax software."
- "It's similar to OCR in that it reads documents, but the goal is a full first draft with source-linked review, not just extracting a few fields."

## 7. Demo The Same Story Every Time

Goal: show one coherent return workflow, then branch only when the prospect's pain requires it.

Core demo sequence:
- Documents: show that Cranston accepts PDFs, spreadsheets, scans, images, Word docs, prior returns, and support files.
- Findings: show missing docs, wrong-year docs, duplicate docs, missing EINs, handwritten items, and tax opportunities.
- Draft: show line-by-line fields that will be entered into the return.
- Source evidence: show where a number came from.
- Year-over-year: show sanity checks against the prior return.
- Export: explain what lands in Drake, Lacerte, or the supported destination.
- Review control: show how the preparer or reviewer can dismiss, resolve, edit, or proceed.

Ask during the demo:
- "Does that interface make sense?"
- "Does that workflow map to how your team reviews returns?"
- "Would you want to check the findings before export, or push drafts faster and review downstream?"
- "Do you want the admin to upload documents and hand off at findings, or should preparers own that first pass?"
- "Is this the kind of view your reviewer would trust?"

## 8. Handle Product Boundaries Directly

Goal: build trust by being precise.

Use these answer patterns:
- Supported forms: "Right now we support 1040, 1065, 1120, and 1120S."
- Tax software: "We integrate with Drake, and support for Lacerte depends on the environment. We'll confirm the exact setup before the test."
- Hosted environments: "We work with hosted environments, but setup means coordinating with the hosting provider."
- TaxDome: "You can keep TaxDome. Cranston can layer into the prep workflow instead of forcing a portal migration."
- AI security: "We use enterprise API subscriptions where customer data is not used to train the base models."
- Unknown technical request: "Let me take that back to the tech team and verify what the API allows."

Questions to use when boundaries come up:
- "Which exact environment are you in?"
- "Who controls installation?"
- "What part of the app creates that data?"
- "Is there a specific endpoint or action we would need from the platform?"
- "Would you be able to show us the current workflow on the next call?"

## 9. Qualify The Trial Or Pilot

Goal: turn interest into a concrete proof step using their own returns.

Ask:
- "Does this look like something that might be a fit for you?"
- "Do you have extensions you're still working on?"
- "Would you want to set up a time where we can provision an account and test a return?"
- "Would you be able to find a couple returns that you'd like to test?"
- "Could you use one that's moderately complex and familiar to you?"
- "Would you recommend using one you've already done so we can compare output?"
- "If this works, would you be able to roll it out for more returns?"
- "Who should be trained first: one preparer, two preparers, an admin, or the reviewer?"

Test-return guidance:
- Use a completed, reviewed return first so the team can compare against a known answer.
- Choose moderate complexity, not the hardest unresolved return in the firm.
- Pick something representative of the workflow they actually want to improve.
- Include the person who will operate the system and the person who will trust or review the output.

Pilot language:
- "First, let's provision an account and upload one or two returns together."
- "The model takes about 20 minutes to run, so we can upload together and then schedule a separate output-review call."
- "If the first return looks good, we can pilot a defined batch, maybe 25 to 50 extension returns, then decide how broadly to roll it out next season."

## 10. Close With A Calendar Step

Goal: never end with vague interest. Close on a dated next action.

Ask:
- "Would next week work for you?"
- "What time range works for you and the reviewer?"
- "What is the other stakeholder's email?"
- "Do you think you can find a couple returns between now and then?"
- "If questions come up about what kind of return to pick, can you send me an email?"

Close by confirming:
- Date and time.
- Who is attending.
- What they will bring.
- What Cranston will do before the call.
- What success looks like on the next call.

## The Max Question Bank

Use these in roughly this order.

Opening:
- "How are you?"

- "I'm excited to learn more about you and your practice."
- "You run a full-service firm, is that right?"

- "How many tax returns do you do in an average year?"
- "What's the mix of business and personal?"
- "What forms do you do most?"
- "How many people are on the team?"
- "Do you use offshore support?"

Workflow:
- "What tax software do you use?"
- "Do you use a practice management tool or portal?"
- "How do clients get documents to you?"
- "Are you hosted?"
- "Who prepares, who reviews, and who handles intake?"

Pain:
- "What are you trying to automate?"
- "Tell me about your search for capacity."
- "What have you tried so far?"
- "What did you like or not like?"

- "What would need to be true for this to save time?"

Demo checks:
- "Does that make sense?"
- "Can you see my screen?"
- "Does that interface make sense?"
- "Would your reviewer trust this view?"
- "Is this how your team thinks about the return?"

Pilot:
- "Does this look like it might be a fit?"
- "Do you have extensions still open?"
- "Can we test on one or two real returns?"
- "Can you pick one that's moderately complex and already familiar?"
- "Who should be on the output-review call?"
- "If it works, how many returns would you want to pilot?"

## What Makes The Flow Work

- Max earns the demo by asking about the firm first.
- He asks concrete operating questions, not generic pain questions.
- He makes informed guesses and lets the prospect correct him.
- He positions Cranston against the job to be done: capacity, review leverage, source traceability, and fewer low-value clicks.
- He answers boundaries honestly and turns unknowns into follow-up actions.
- He closes on a test using the prospect's own returns, not a vague "let us know."
