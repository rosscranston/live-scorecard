# Before the First Call: Qualification and Attendance Plan

## The call should only happen when these are true

- We know the firm type, location, core services, and approximate team size.
- We have a credible estimate of annual tax-return volume and the mix of individual versus business work.
- We know the prospect's role, whether they influence the buying decision, and who else needs to attend (owner, tax leader, operations lead, reviewer, or the person who will run the workflow).
- We understand their current tax software, hosting setup, client portal/practice-management tools, and whether a technical or integration constraint is likely.
- The prospect has named a specific workflow pain and why it matters now: capacity, data entry, review bottlenecks, missing client information, quality/rework, or a tax-season deadline.
- The prospect is open to evaluating a practical next step if there is a fit—not merely gathering general AI information.
- The calendar invite has a confirmed date, time zone, attendee list, and clear purpose. The meeting owner has a phone number and email for the primary contact.

If any of these are unknown, do lightweight research or a short qualification exchange before the meeting. Do not use the first call to discover basic facts that could have been confirmed in advance.

## If the calendar invite has not been accepted

Treat a pending invite as unconfirmed. Be helpful and specific, never passive-aggressive.

1. **Immediately after booking:** Send the invite with a title that states the value of the call, a concise agenda, and the names/roles of expected attendees. Ask the contact to accept the invite and forward it to anyone who should weigh in.
2. **Two business days before:** Send a short personal note from the meeting owner: restate the outcome, name the one preparation item that will make the call useful, and ask for a quick confirmation or a better time.
3. **One business day before:** If the invite is still pending, send a brief email and call or text only if the contact previously provided or consented to that channel. Ask a yes/no question: “Are we still on for [day/time]?” Offer an easy reschedule link or two alternatives.
4. **Day of:** If there is still no confirmation, send a final concise reminder 2–3 hours beforehand. Do not over-message. If there is no response and no attendance after a short grace period, mark it as a no-show and send one warm rescheduling follow-up.

Suggested pending-invite note:

> Hi [Name]—looking forward to [day] at [time/time zone]. We’ll focus on how Cranston could help with [their stated workflow/pain], and we can confirm whether it fits your [tax software/workflow]. Could you accept the invite or let me know if a different time is easier? If [reviewer/owner] will evaluate the output, it would be great to include them.

When the slot is in demand, use clear, courteous urgency:

> Hi [Name]—we have other people asking for this time, so please confirm the calendar invite by [deadline]. If I do not hear from you, I will release the slot and we can find another time that works.

## Increase the show rate

- Book around a concrete problem the prospect has already acknowledged; the meeting title and reminder should repeat that problem in their words.
- Give the call a clear, modest promise: “assess fit for your workflow,” not “generic product demo.”
- Make the next action easy: calendar hold immediately, time zone explicit, one-click reschedule, and no heavy pre-work.
- Ask during booking who must be present for a useful conversation, then invite them directly rather than relying on the primary contact to relay context.
- Send a short reminder with an agenda and a reason to attend—not just “looking forward to it.”
- Where appropriate, ask the prospect to bring one representative completed return or workflow example. This creates a commitment and makes the call materially more valuable.
- Keep the scheduled length proportional to the promise. If discovery is all that is needed, use a short meeting; reserve longer time for a tailored workflow review or test-return session.
- Log the booking source, stated pain, attendee roles, invite status, reminder attempts, and outcome. Review no-shows by source, persona, firm size, and lead time so the team can improve the process with evidence.

## Confirm the prospect is true ICP before investing in a deep demo

The strongest early-fit signal is a tax or accounting firm with meaningful, repeatable return-preparation work, a real capacity or review bottleneck, and a workflow Cranston can support today.

### Required qualification areas

| Area | What to confirm | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Firm and buyer | Firm type, team size, primary contact's role, decision maker, and future users | Ensures the right people can assess and buy |
| Work volume | Annual return count, seasonal pressure, and 1040/entity mix | Establishes whether the problem is repeated and valuable |
| Workflow pain | Exact bottleneck, current owner, and cost of the problem | Keeps the conversation tied to a real use case |
| Technical corridor | Tax software, edition/hosting, portal, and relevant integrations | Prevents promising a workflow we cannot support |
| Urgency and process | Why now, current alternatives, evaluation timeline, and next decision | Distinguishes active evaluation from curiosity |
| Pilot readiness | Ability to test representative completed returns and include an operator/reviewer | Creates a credible route to proof and adoption |

### Disqualify or re-route early when

- The firm has little recurring tax-preparation volume or no defined workflow to improve.
- The contact cannot identify a meaningful pain, urgency, or person who owns the outcome.
- The core request falls outside current product support and no reasonable investigation/pilot path exists.
- The prospect wants only a broad AI education session with no plausible evaluation or next step.

When fit is uncertain, state the boundary plainly and use the first conversation to verify it: “Before we schedule a deeper demo, I want to make sure your software environment and the work you want to automate are in our supported corridor.”

## Internal pre-call handoff

Before the meeting owner joins, the CRM or call brief should contain:

- Account, website, firm type, location, and known team size.
- Contact roles, decision maker, attendees, phone numbers, and invite acceptance status.
- Lead source and every relevant prior email/conversation, including what was promised.
- Return volume, return mix, current tax software, hosting, portal, and known integrations.
- Stated pain, buying trigger, competitor/tools considered, and likely objections.
- Meeting objective, agenda, desired next step, and the one or two questions that must be answered on the call.

## Review-call goal

After reviewing the prospect's second return, the goal is to earn a commitment to a trial or pilot.

Frame the review around the prospect's workflow, confirm that the output addresses the concerns from the first return, and close on a defined next step: a small, representative pilot batch with agreed participants, returns, timeline, and success criteria.

## Test-project goal

Ask the prospect to select a completed return where they already know the correct answer. It should be complicated enough—and representative enough of the returns they normally process—that a strong result gives them confidence Cranston can handle the level of complexity they need.

Avoid an unusually simple return that cannot prove value, as well as the hardest unresolved edge case that cannot fairly test the core workflow. Include the source documents and the person who prepared or reviewed the original return so the team can compare the output against a known result.

## First-call success criterion: build conviction through a meaningful test set

The strongest review-call conviction came from prospects who ran three to four test returns—not just one—and included a messy or anomalous 1040. A useful test set should include representative normal returns plus one “garbage” or unusually difficult 1040, so the prospect sees that the system can produce useful answers and surface real issues even when the input is imperfect.

By the end of the first call, get agreement that the prospect will bring:

- Three to four representative, completed tax returns they know well enough to evaluate.
- The relevant source documents for each return.
- One anomalous, messy, or document-heavy 1040 that tests the edge cases they care about.
- The preparer or reviewer who can validate the output and explain any expected exceptions.

Suggested close:

> For the best test, come with three to four representative tax returns and one anomaly—something messy or unusual—so you can see the system handle the kind of complexity you deal with in real life. It can surface issues in the return as well as produce a strong first draft. If it misses something, you can ask it to take a second look at a document; and as we run more examples from your firm, we can refine the workflow and instructions around how your team works.

## Questions for Max

- **Laura call:** You had been emailing Laura before the first call. What exactly were you sending—messages, resources, questions, or a meeting invite?
- Did Laura email you directly, or did the conversation begin through another channel or teammate?
- Which of those pre-call touches seemed to create enough interest for her to book and attend?
- At what point did you know Laura was a strong fit, and which facts were still unknown when the call began?
