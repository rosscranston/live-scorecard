# Anthropic-style notebook channels for Cranston AI

## Bottom line

Cranston should pilot a lightweight version of Anthropic’s notebook-channel pattern for six weeks: one public internal `#notebook-[name]` channel per person who owns a meaningful area, a small index channel, a weekly “what I’m doing / learning / unsure about” post, and a separate durable decision record. The pattern is worth testing because it can make leadership reasoning, goals, uncertainty, and dissent easier to discover without adding meetings. It should not be copied as a mandate to publish every thought or as a substitute for formal product, customer, security, or personnel records.

## What this research establishes

- Anthropic publicly describes “notebook channels” as internal Slack channels used as personal feeds for thoughts, work, priorities, and provocative ideas.
- Anthropic’s Head of Product Ami Vora described a weekly cadence of goals, results against goals, product thinking, and places where teams need clarity.
- Anthropic’s Head of Growth Amol Avasare described public disagreement with leadership, including disagreement with CEO Dario Amodei, as encouraged.
- Anthropic’s own human-agent-team guidance explicitly recommends defaulting new communication channels to public internally, documenting decisions in searchable artifacts, and treating written context as essential for agents.
- Slack itself does not appear to offer a special “Notebook” object. The reported pattern is implemented using ordinary public channels, channel topic/purpose, threads, pins, canvases/tabs, notifications, and optionally workflows or API automation.
- The direct evidence is not an independent evaluation of notebook channels. The details come primarily from Anthropic leaders’ public interviews and Anthropic/Slack customer-marketing materials, supplemented by research on enterprise-social-media visibility and psychological safety.
