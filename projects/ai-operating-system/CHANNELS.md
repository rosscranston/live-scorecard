# Channels

## Webinar channel playbook

**Owner:** Cranston Growth / Sales
**Primary presenter:** Max Minsker
**Purpose:** Increase qualified top-of-funnel volume and replace repeatable first-call education and light qualification with a one-to-many sales motion.
**Primary conversion:** A qualified attendee books a firm-specific second demo with the operator and reviewer who would evaluate Cranston.
**Status:** Pilot playbook. Assumptions and targets should be revised after three live webinars.

---

## 1. The decision

Cranston should run a live, recurring, 50-minute webinar that combines the repeatable parts of the first sales call with the most persuasive part of the second call: an end-to-end, source-linked product walkthrough. Max presents. The rest of the team works the chat, answers approved questions, identifies fit, and books interested attendees into a separate firm-specific demo.

The webinar is not a group discovery call and should not try to close every attendee. Its job is to:

1. Teach CPAs what Cranston does in the context of their current workflow.
2. Collect enough structured information to segment the room without exposing firm details.
3. Demonstrate one coherent return from source package to professional review.
4. Make clear who Cranston is and is not a fit for.
5. Convert qualified interest into a scheduled second demo with a concrete proof plan.

The second demo remains one-to-one because software edition, hosting, form mix, workflow, security diligence, and proof-return selection require firm-specific discussion. The webinar should remove repeated explanation from that meeting so the second demo can begin with the prospect's actual workflow and test criteria.

### Governing hypothesis

A recurring webinar will create more qualified conversations per hour than repeating the same first-call education individually, provided that Cranston keeps the topic narrow, uses live polls to replace basic discovery, demonstrates reviewer control rather than a feature tour, and makes booking a firm-specific proof session the only primary call to action.

### What would disprove the hypothesis

Pause or redesign the channel if, after three comparable events:

- qualified second demos per Max-hour are not better than the current one-to-one intro-call baseline;
- booked attendees are materially less qualified or have lower held-meeting rates;
- the mixed audience forces so much generic explanation that high-intent attendees do not progress;
- chat and follow-up capacity cannot support timely, accurate responses; or
- the event repeatedly creates unsupported expectations about integrations, security, forms, or implementation.

## 3. The flagship webinar

### Working title

**From client package to review-ready return: See how Cranston fits into a CPA firm's existing tax workflow**

### Subtitle

See one return move from prior-year context and source documents through missing-item review, field-level evidence, professional judgment, and the tax software your firm already uses.

### The one promise

By the end, attendees should understand what work Cranston performs, what the professional still controls, where the result lands, and what their own firm would need to test next.

### Who it is for

- Firms constrained by preparer capacity or reviewer throughput.
- Teams spending professional time on document intake, manual entry, source checking, or avoidable review cleanup.
- Owners considering AI tax-prep tools but unwilling to give up professional judgment or their existing tax software.
- Firms that can identify an operator, a reviewer, and a representative return for a controlled test.

### Who it is not for

- Consumers looking for tax filing help.
- People seeking a fully autonomous product that replaces the signing professional.
- Firms expecting every form, tax software, hosting environment, or integration to be supported without technical qualification.
- Anyone who wants a production integration demonstrated before the exact environment and permissions have been verified.

## 5. Live poll and branching design

Use five polls. Ask the first four before the core demo and the fifth immediately before the CTA. Max should acknowledge the distribution and explain the relevant branches without attempting individual discovery in public.

### Poll 1 — Your role

**Question:** Which best describes your role in the tax workflow?

- Owner or partner
- Tax leader or reviewer
- Preparer
- Operations or admin
- Technology or other

**How Max responds:**

“Great. I am going to show the same return through four lenses. Owners should watch for capacity and control. Preparers should watch what manual work disappears and where judgment remains. Reviewers should watch the source trail and exceptions. Operations teams should watch intake and handoffs.”

### Poll 2 — Tax software

**Question:** What is your firm's primary tax software today?

- Drake
- Lacerte
- CCH Axcess
- UltraTax
- ProConnect or ProSeries
- Other or evaluating a switch

**How Max responds:**

Do not turn the poll into an integration promise. Say:

“The workflow I am showing is consistent, but the exact destination step depends on the software, edition, hosting environment, and installation permissions. We will speak to the main patterns today and confirm your exact corridor in a firm-specific session.”

### Poll 3 — Who would use Cranston?

**Question:** If your firm adopted a tool like this, who would operate it most often?

- An admin or intake coordinator
- A preparer
- A reviewer or tax leader
- The owner/partner
- Multiple roles or not sure yet

**How Max responds:**

- **Admin:** Emphasize upload, completeness, obvious missing items, and clean handoff. Make clear that admins should not be asked to make tax judgments.
- **Preparer:** Emphasize package analysis, draft fields, source links, resolving exceptions, and less repetitive entry.
- **Reviewer:** Emphasize prioritization, year-over-year variance, exact evidence, diagnostics, and approval control.
- **Owner:** Emphasize capacity, standardization, the ability to keep the current stack, and measuring time/rework on a controlled pilot.
- **Multiple roles:** Explain the recommended handoff: admin loads and checks completeness; preparer resolves and prepares; reviewer handles exceptions and signs off.

### Poll 4 — Main bottleneck

**Question:** Where does a normal return lose the most time in your firm?

- Collecting or correcting client documents
- Entering and organizing data
- Preparing workpapers and the first draft
- Reviewer cleanup and source checking
- Moving work between systems

**How Max responds:**

Call out the top two results. During the demo, spend one extra minute on each and explicitly state where Cranston helps and where a human or existing system remains responsible.

### Poll 5 — Desired next step

**Question:** What would be most useful after today's session?

- A firm-specific workflow demo
- A test using a familiar completed return
- A technical fit check for our software/hosting
- Security or implementation materials
- Just send the recording for now

**How the team responds:**

- Anyone selecting one of the first four receives a direct, role-appropriate chat message from one assigned team member.
- “Recording only” enters nurture unless their questions or chat behavior show higher intent.
- Never have multiple teammates message the same attendee. The chat captain owns assignment.

## 6. Run of show and Max's talk track

### 0:00-0:03 — Welcome and outcome

**Objective:** Establish who the session is for, what will be shown, and what will not happen.

**Talk track:**

“Thanks for joining. Today I am going to show one tax return from the package arriving through a review-ready draft and the destination-software step. We are not going to pretend every firm has the same workflow. We will use a few polls to understand the room, then I will show how the same system is used differently by an owner, admin, preparer, and reviewer. We are using synthetic data, and we will leave time for questions.”

### 0:03-0:08 — Polls and audience map

Run Polls 1-4. The producer posts the results to Max privately. Max summarizes the top segments and makes one explicit promise to each major role represented.

### 0:08-0:12 — The problem and Cranston's place

**Objective:** Replace the repeatable “why now” section of a first call without making assumptions about an individual attendee.

**Talk track:**

“Across tax firms, the bottleneck is rarely one isolated click. It is the corridor between documents arriving and a reviewer trusting the work: incomplete packages, repetitive preparation, source checking, exceptions, and handoffs between systems. Cranston sits in that corridor. It does not replace the signing professional, and it does not require the webinar audience to reveal a private workflow in public.”

Use the current approved positioning and current public product source. Do not repeat time savings, accuracy, supported-form, integration, customer-count, security, or pricing claims from memory.

### 0:12-0:30 — One-return live demo

The demo must tell one story. Do not narrate every screen.

1. **Introduce the package.** Show the synthetic current-year documents, prior-year return, and one intentionally missing or wrong-year item.
2. **Show what Cranston received.** Explain acceptable source categories only from current approved product documentation.
3. **Show the finding.** Demonstrate how the system identifies the missing/wrong-year evidence or an inconsistency. Explain the difference between a client request, a preparer fix, and a professional judgment.
4. **Follow one number.** Open one field and trace it to its source. This is the proof of reviewability.
5. **Show the draft.** Explain what Cranston proposes to enter and how confidence or exceptions are represented. Do not imply that a preview is already written into the attendee's production environment.
6. **Show year-over-year context.** Use one meaningful variance to demonstrate a review question, not an automatic tax conclusion.
7. **Show professional control.** Resolve, dismiss, edit, or defer one item. State who should make that decision in a real firm.
8. **Show where the result lands.** Demonstrate the approved destination path available in the demo environment. State that the attendee's exact form/software/edition/hosting corridor must be confirmed separately.

At each step answer:

- What did Cranston receive?
- What work did Cranston perform?
- What remains for the professional?
- Where does the result land?

### 0:30-0:37 — Role-based workflow

Use the role poll to describe how the same product fits different people.

**Owner/partner:**

“The question is not whether a screen looks impressive. The question is whether a representative batch reduces elapsed time or rework without weakening review control. That is what we define before a pilot.”

**Admin/intake:**

“The admin can load the package, confirm basic completeness, and route clear missing-item requests. Tax treatment and unresolved exceptions remain with the preparer or reviewer.”

**Preparer:**

“The preparer starts from an organized package, proposed draft, and visible exceptions instead of repeated source hunting and entry. They still resolve ambiguity and apply the firm's tax judgment.”

**Reviewer:**

“The reviewer should not have to trust a black box. The review path is exceptions, year-over-year change, field-level evidence, and the destination return.”

**Technology/operations:**

“The technical fit check covers software, edition, hosting, installation owner, document source, permissions, and rollback before production access.”

### 0:37-0:42 — Fit and proof design

**Objective:** Pre-qualify without pressuring the room.

“A good first test is not the easiest return and not the hardest unresolved return in the firm. It is a completed, reviewed return that is representative of the workflow you want to improve. Include the prior-year context, use a known answer, and bring both the person who will operate the system and the person who must trust the output.”

Explain that the firm-specific session will confirm:

- return type and year;
- software, edition, hosting, and installation ownership;
- current intake and workpaper path;
- operator and reviewer;
- proof return and authorization;
- success criteria; and
- what happens after the test.

### 0:42-0:48 — Moderated Q&A

The chat captain clusters questions into:

1. Workflow and roles.
2. Forms and tax software.
3. Implementation and hosting.
4. Security and data handling.
5. Pricing and commercial terms.
6. Firm-specific questions for private follow-up.

Max answers only from current approved sources. If the answer depends on the firm's environment, say so and route it to the second demo. If an attendee shares sensitive details, the moderator should stop public discussion and invite them to a private follow-up.

### 0:48-0:50 — CTA and booking

Run Poll 5 and show one booking link.

**Talk track:**

“If today's workflow is relevant, the next step is not another generic presentation. It is a 30-minute firm-specific session. We will map your software and roles, choose one familiar completed return, and define what it has to prove. Use the link on screen or reply to the teammate who messages you in chat. If you only want the recording, select that in the poll and we will not force a meeting.”

## 7. Team chat and booking playbook

### Roles for each event

- **Max / presenter:** Delivers the narrative, answers approved product questions, and protects accuracy.
- **Producer:** Controls platform, recording, polls, screen share, timing, backup demo, and incident response.
- **Chat captain:** Assigns attendees to teammates, clusters Q&A, prevents duplicate outreach, and escalates sensitive or unsupported questions.
- **Chat representatives:** Welcome attendees, answer only from approved material, ask one private qualifying question when useful, and book second demos.
- **Calendar/follow-up owner:** Confirms bookings, required attendees, preparation instructions, and follow-up completion.
- **Analyst:** Exports event data, reconciles it to the CRM, calculates the scorecard, and records lessons.

### Chat rules

1. One Cranston owner per attendee.
2. Do not paste generic sales messages into every private chat.
3. Answer public questions publicly when the answer helps the room and is approved.
4. Move firm-specific software, hosting, client, security, pricing, or implementation details to private chat or follow-up.
5. Never ask for taxpayer data, passwords, production access, or client documents in webinar chat.
6. Never state that a meeting is booked until the calendar confirmation is verified.
7. Preserve substantive unanswered questions for follow-up; do not invent an answer to keep the conversation moving.

### Opening public chat message

“Welcome. Tell us your role and the workflow question you most want answered. Please do not share taxpayer or client information. We will answer general questions here and route firm-specific questions privately.”

### Private message for a high-intent attendee

“You mentioned **[their stated software/role/bottleneck]**. That is best handled in a firm-specific session because the answer depends on the exact workflow. Would it be useful to map the setup and choose one familiar completed return for a controlled test? I can send the booking link.”

### Private message for a technical-fit question

“We should verify that rather than generalize from the webinar. The fit check covers the exact software, edition, hosting environment, installation owner, form/year, and desired write path. If you send only those non-client details, I can make sure the right person joins the follow-up.”

### Private message after a trial hand-raise

“A fair first test uses one completed, reviewed return that your operator and reviewer know well. If you book here, please invite both roles if possible. We will confirm authorization and the exact test corridor before any documents are accepted.”

### Booking confirmation requirements

The meeting is not fully qualified until the follow-up owner records:

- confirmed date and time;
- firm and attendees;
- each attendee's role;
- primary tax software, edition if known, and hosting/local environment;
- approximate return volume and mix;
- primary bottleneck;
- whether the session is workflow demo, technical fit check, or proof setup;
- what the prospect should bring; and
- the one-sentence win condition.

## 8. Lead scoring and follow-up

The score is a routing tool, not a substitute for judgment. Do not inflate a lead because they asked many questions if the product corridor is not verified.

### Engagement score

| Signal | Points |
|---|---:|
| Requests a firm-specific demo, test, technical fit check, or security review | +5 |
| Books a second demo during or within 24 hours of the event | +5 |
| Asks a specific question about workflow, proof, implementation, integration, or buying process | +3 |
| Attends at least 75% of the live event | +2 |
| Completes at least four polls | +2 |
| Registration indicates target decision-maker, operator, or reviewer role | +2 |
| Brings another stakeholder from the same firm | +2 |
| Clear software/form/hosting mismatch requiring unplanned product work | -3 pending verification |
| Consumer/non-firm attendee or no relevant workflow | -5 |

### Routing

- **Hot — 8+ points or explicit meeting/test request:** Personal follow-up within one business hour. Attempt to book the correct meeting type; do not repeat the generic demo.
- **Warm — 4-7 points:** Personal follow-up within one business day using their role, software, bottleneck, or question. Offer the appropriate second step.
- **Nurture — 0-3 points:** Send recording/recap and the next relevant webinar. Do not send a sequence pretending they requested a demo.
- **Needs verification:** Assign an owner and verify the exact product/security/technical question before advancing or promising fit.

1. Confirm the firm's current workflow and the attendee's poll/registration answers.

6. Agree on the upload/test meeting and the separate output-review meeting.
7. Put both meetings on the calendar before ending when the prospect agrees.

## 9. Funnel, scorecard, and economics

Track one cohort from invitation source through activated proof. Registrations alone are not success.

```text
Invited audience
  -> registration
  -> live attendance
  -> meaningful engagement
  -> qualified hand-raise
  -> booked second demo
  -> held second demo
  -> proof/pilot agreed
  -> representative return activated
  -> commercial conversion
```

### Required metrics

- invites and registrations by source;
- landing-page conversion by source when measurable;
- registration-to-live-attendance rate;
- average attendance duration;
- poll completion and chat participation;
- qualified hand-raises;
- second demos booked during event, within 24 hours, and within seven days;
- booked-to-held rate;
- held-demo-to-proof/pilot rate;
- proof-to-activated-return rate;
- activated-return-to-commercial conversion;
- Max live/prep hours and total team hours;
- qualified held demos per Max-hour;
- cost per qualified held demo by source; and
- reasons for no-fit, no-show, or no-progress.

### Pilot operating targets

These are internal starting thresholds, not external industry benchmarks.

| Metric | Initial target after three events |
|---|---:|
| Registration-to-live-attendance | 40%+ |
| Live attendees completing four or more polls | 60%+ |
| Live attendees with meaningful engagement | 40%+ |
| Live attendees booking a qualified second demo within 7 days | 15%+ |
| Booked second demos that are held | 75%+ |
| Held second demos that agree to a defined proof step | 40%+ |
| Follow-up completed within SLA | 95%+ |
| Unsupported claims made live | 0 |

The most important comparison is **qualified held demos per Max-hour versus one-to-one intro calls**. If the webinar produces more registrations but not more qualified held demos or proofs, the channel is not working.

### Experiment rules

- Run the same core webinar three times before making a major format change.
- Change only one major variable per event after the baseline: title/topic, time, audience source, co-host, or CTA.
- Preserve source attribution so partner, outbound, inbound, conference, referral, and paid cohorts can be compared.
- Review losses and no-fit reasons, not just conversion wins.
- Do not automate an evergreen event until live Q&A and conversion patterns are stable.

## 10. Six-week pilot plan

### Week 0 — Build

- Confirm the approved product, pricing, security, integration, and implementation sources.
- Build the synthetic demo return and a transparent backup recording.
- Configure registration fields, five polls, consent, booking links, and attendee export.
- Create the invitation, reminder, follow-up, and chat macros.
- Define CRM fields and the event-source naming convention.
- Create a shared real-time Q&A document with approved answers and owners.

### Week 1 — Rehearse

- Run a full timed rehearsal with Max, producer, and chat team.
- Test the primary and backup demo path.
- Seed difficult questions about software, hosted environments, forms, security, pricing, and professional responsibility.
- Confirm that Max can finish the core narrative in 42 minutes before Q&A/CTA.
- Verify that no real taxpayer, prospect, or attendee data appears on screen.

### Week 2 — Webinar 1: Baseline

- Audience: warm pipeline, inbound leads, demo no-shows, and team network.
- Use the flagship title and standard time.
- Debrief within 24 hours.
- Do not change the format based on one loud comment; separate patterns from anecdotes.

### Week 3 — Follow through

- Hold booked second demos.
- Track qualification, attendance, proof progression, and no-fit reasons.
- Repair only material accuracy, logistics, or conversion failures.

### Week 4 — Webinar 2: Repeat

- Keep title, run of show, polls, and CTA stable.
- Add one improved explanation based on repeated unanswered questions.
- Consider one guest practitioner only if approved and rehearsed; do not let a co-host turn the event into an unscripted case study.

### Week 5 — Follow through and compare

- Compare sources and cohorts.
- Calculate qualified held demos per Max-hour.
- Select one controlled variable for Webinar 3 only if the first two runs reveal a clear hypothesis.

### Week 6 — Webinar 3 and go/no-go

- Run the controlled test.
- Decide: stop, revise, continue biweekly, or scale to weekly.
- Document the winning title, audience, talk track, poll mix, CTA, and follow-up process.
- Create the next topic only after the flagship webinar's unit economics are credible.

## 11. Topic roadmap after the flagship works

Keep each webinar about one job, buyer, or proof. Do not combine all products into one monthly update.

1. **Flagship:** From client package to review-ready return.
2. **1040 workflow:** Missing documents, prior-year comparison, findings, source trail, and draft review.
3. **Business-return workflow:** P&L/balance sheet/trial balance intake, workpapers, exceptions, and destination handoff. Publish only from current supported functionality.
4. **Reviewer session:** How a reviewer inspects exceptions, year-over-year change, source evidence, and professional decisions.
5. **Owner capacity session:** How to design a controlled proof and measure preparation/review leverage without weakening controls.
6. **Operations session:** Where admins, preparers, and reviewers hand work off—and what each role should never be asked to decide.
7. **Partner webinar:** A complete workflow with a relevant hosting, practice-management, or tax-technology partner.

Juno's webinar page is a useful competitive benchmark because it uses a narrow product announcement, a scheduled agenda, an end-to-end workflow, a live return demo, Q&A, and a direct registration CTA. Cranston should match that clarity and differentiate through live segmentation, explicit role-based workflows, transparent fit boundaries, source-linked reviewer control, and a better-qualified second-demo handoff.

## 12. Risk controls and failure modes

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| The room is too mixed for a useful story | One flagship job, registration segmentation, five polls, and role-based branches. Move individual complexity to the second demo. |
| The webinar becomes a generic feature tour | One return, one source trail, one exception, one professional decision, one destination step. |
| Max makes a time-sensitive or unsupported claim | Live approved-answer sheet; chat captain flags questions; verify rather than improvise; refresh current sources before every event. |
| An integration is implied from a software poll | Always qualify form, software, edition, hosting, permissions, and workflow separately. |
| Real taxpayer or prospect data appears | Synthetic demo tenant, clean browser profile, notifications off, pre-event screen audit, backup recording. |
| Live demo fails | Rehearsed backup recording/screenshots; state transparently that the live environment failed; do not imply a recording is live. |
| Chat team duplicates or pressures attendees | One owner per attendee, chat-captain assignment, intent-based scripts, visible opt-out. |
| Questions overwhelm Max | Cluster Q&A; answer the highest-value general questions; assign every unresolved question an owner and follow-up deadline. |
| High-intent accounts are slowed by the webinar | Preserve a direct-to-discovery path for urgent referrals, strategic accounts, and active technical evaluations. |
| A registrant shares sensitive data | Moderator interrupts, removes/hides the content if possible, moves them to a private approved channel, and follows incident policy. |
| Recording creates privacy issues | Explicit recording notice and consent; exclude private chat from distributed recording; redact attendee information from clips. |
| The team celebrates vanity metrics | Evaluate qualified held demos, proofs, activated returns, and qualified meetings per Max-hour. |
| Follow-up breaks at scale | Do not increase cadence until 95% of follow-up is completed within SLA and the team can support booked meetings. |

### Pre-event claim check

Before every live event, one owner must re-verify:

- product workflow and supported functionality;
- supported forms and tax years;
- tax-software integrations, editions, and hosting qualifications;
- pricing and minimums if mentioned;
- security, data location, model-training, retention, subprocessors, and SOC 2 status;
- implementation and trial terms; and
- any customer proof, performance number, or case study.

Current public Cranston language says the product works alongside existing tax software and that the professional remains responsible for review/sign-off. The public security page states that the SOC 2 Type II examination is in progress. Do not say Cranston is SOC 2 certified unless a later approved source confirms completion.

## 14. Definition of done for each webinar

An event is complete only when:

- the recording and attendance export are stored in the approved location;
- every registrant has one source, status, engagement score, and follow-up owner;
- every booked meeting has been verified on the calendar;
- all explicit questions have an approved answer or named owner/deadline;
- no attendee received duplicate personal outreach;
- follow-up was completed within SLA;
- the scorecard includes qualified held demos per Max-hour;
- unsupported claims or incidents are documented and corrected before reuse; and
- one short debrief records what to keep, change, and test next.

### 15-minute debrief questions

1. Which audience segment leaned in, and what evidence shows it?
2. Where did Max have to repeat or clarify the story?
3. Which poll best predicted a booked/held second demo?
4. Which questions should become approved enablement material?
5. Did any answer overstate current product, integration, security, pricing, or implementation facts?
6. Did the second-demo CTA produce the right meeting, attendees, and proof plan?
7. What single change has the strongest evidence for the next event?
